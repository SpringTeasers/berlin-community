About the town

About Berlin

A small Connecticut town that measures itself by its center — Berlin sits at the geographic middle of the state, and it has been a working, growing community for more than two centuries.

History & heritage

Berlin was incorporated in 1785, making it one of Connecticut’s older towns. It began life under a different name: the Great Swamp Society, a designation that recalls the wetland landscape the early settlement grew around.

What shaped Berlin after that was work. The town’s early economy rested on agriculture and industry — most distinctively the tin industry and brick manufacturing. Those trades gave Berlin its first identity as a place that made things, and they set the pattern the town still follows today: a community where residential neighborhoods and industrial employers sit side by side.

Berlin’s institutions have kept pace with its growth. In 1995–1996, a charter revision reorganized town government into the Town Council–Town Manager system that Berlin still uses, replacing the older structure and creating a governance model built around day-to-day responsiveness to residents.

Government

Berlin is governed under a Town Council–Town Manager system, adopted in the 1995–1996 charter revision. In practical terms, that model puts an elected Town Council in charge of policy and a professional Town Manager in charge of running the town’s day-to-day operations — a structure the town adopted specifically to improve governance and make it more responsive to residents.

Because this site is a guide rather than a government record, the details that change — the current council members, office hours, meeting schedules, and official notices — live on the official town website. That is the source to check and to cite.
